## Summary

riskscope is a free, open-source (AGPL-3.0) web security scanner. It grades a website's security posture
and returns a prioritised, plain-language list of fixes with copy-paste configuration. I would like to make
it useful to ANSI's mission of protecting Tunisia's digital infrastructure, in a way that respects the law and
the authority that only a national CERT holds.

## The problem

Many Tunisian websites — small businesses, associations, public bodies — have avoidable security gaps:
missing HTTPS or expiring certificates, absent security headers, exposed `.git`/`.env` files, dangling
subdomains open to takeover, and out-of-date software (for example the October 2026 critical PHP flaws). Their
operators usually don't know, and don't have a security team.

## Why this needs ANSI, not a private actor

Broad scanning and notification of systems across the country is lawful only for the body mandated to do it.
A private individual scanning address space they don't own would be breaking the law, however good their
intent. ANSI/tunCERT is the appropriate authority. riskscope is offered as a tool for that authority, not as a
way around it.

## What I can offer

1. **A self-hosted riskscope instance under ANSI's control**, scoped to the ranges ANSI is authorised to
   assess, with results flowing to ANSI's notification process.
2. **Coordinated-disclosure tooling** that works from already-published internet-scan data (Shodan, Censys,
   Rapid7 Open Data) — identifying exposed Tunisian organisations and drafting notifications — with no scanning
   traffic generated by us.
3. **A public self-check service** (already live) that any Tunisian site owner can use on their own site,
   which ANSI could link to from awareness campaigns.
4. **Open source and free**, so there is no vendor lock-in and ANSI's team can audit and extend it.

## Suggested next step

A short call to see whether (1) a scoped instance, (2) the disclosure tooling, or (3) linking the public
self-check from an ANSI awareness page would be most useful. I can demo any of them.

## About riskscope

- Source: https://github.com/alifjegham-wq/Riskscope
- Checks: TLS/certificates, security headers, cookies, CORS, SPF/DMARC, DNS hardening, subdomain takeover,
  exposed files, outdated front-end libraries, PHP version against critical advisories, and (for verified
  owners) risky open ports.
- Safety by design: refuses private/internal addresses, caps IP-range scans, and gates intrusive checks behind
  proof of domain ownership.
